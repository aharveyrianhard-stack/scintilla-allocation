1 · THE FRAME, IN TEN LINESthe process, one sentence a step — what goes in, what comes out

  1. Read how the eleven sectors stand.in: five readings per sector (the State Street fund's Geiger, the Hub's sector compare, the served-set bow tie, the tree's close tier, the sector ranking) · out: one number from −1 to +1, a standing (washed out / middling / stretched) and a direction (improving / leading / pulling back / breaking down).
  2. Pick where to look: the washed-out sectors, and the strong sectors whose leaders are pulling back.in: the standings · out: two or three sectors, with the reason in the numbers.
  3. List what is in the sector in our universe.in: the Hub's cohorts and the tree · out: the parent fund, the sub-funds, and every company the Hub serves under that sector, grouped by business.
  4. For each company, find the peers that actually share its business.in: FMP revenue segments (else the FMP industry), the peers FMP and Massive name · out: the 12 nearest comparables, and among them the ones in the same business — only those set the price when there are four or more; otherwise the whole set does.
  5. Leave out the outliers.in: each peer's multiples against the group on a log scale · out: a peer priced far from the group on three or more yardsticks is out of every median (live: far on either side; the 7 Oct study: far above only).
  6. Price the company on six yardsticks.in: trailing and forward P/E, EV/EBITDA, EV/sales, P/S, PEG (P/FFO for property trusts); each peer's multiple × the company's own figure · out: a price per peer per yardstick, and each yardstick's median price.
  7. Let growth in.in: earnings growth into the following year, the PEG yardstick · out: a faster grower than its peers is credited through PEG (live, at PEG's fitted weight; the study caps growth at 30% a year and makes it the heaviest yardstick).
  8. Draw the football field.in: every peer-by-yardstick price, weighted by how well each yardstick fits the set · out: the centre (the weighted median), the range (the middle half), today's price on it, and the upside to the centre in percent — beside it the channel position (technical) and the analyst median (a third opinion).
  9. Rank the names inside the sector and cut the short list.in: upside to the centre, whether the set is its own business, whether the centre is fragile, growth, pre-profit or not · out: the sector's short list, each name with one sentence.
  10. Put the short list against another sector's.in: both lists, both sectors' direction, channel room, analysts · out: what to look at first today, and what would flip it.

5 · WHERE THE METHOD IS WEAK, SAID PLAINLYpeers that are not really comparable · names with no earnings · estimates we do not trust · a median of three · and, for each example, the line Alan would most likely push back on
