WHAT COULD BE WRONG — AT THE OPEN AND AFTER THE CLOSE

  • The first minutes after 09:30. The chart API's quotes for the new session can arrive a minute or two after the bell, and HYG prints thinly at first; until SPY's quote carries today's session the page shows the last close with "at the close of … New York is not trading". A pre-market HYG quote can sit a quarter of a percent under the close and move the number a point before the open, as it did on 8 October.
  • Today's own high and low. The VIX's intraday high comes from the macro board; a touch of 20 counts half a step, so a high the board missed is 1.5 points of the account at most.
  • After 16:00. The page keeps reading every five minutes until 20:00 and once more from 16:10 for the settled daily bars; the number can step by a few tenths at 16:10 when the day's bar settles. Over the weekend it says so and reads again on Monday.
  • If a rule file fails to load (a bad deploy, a blocked file) the page still reads and says which version it is showing: version 2 without DS3's file, version 1 without DS2's.
  • The hour average in the long version and this minute's number on the first screen can differ by a few tenths; both say which they are.
  • A quote that fails never blanks the screen: the last reading stays with its age and the cause, as everywhere in this tool.
  • A new browser starts at $500K and 25 : 75 and shows "reading live prices…" for about a second; a browser that has been here before shows its last number, dimmed, until the first live read lands.

WHAT WAS NOT DONE

  • Not pushed, not deployed: the coordinator merges to main (push main deploys). The branch and SHA are in the return.
  • The five-minute path in the long version's chart reads the VIX as it last read, not minute by minute (the five-minute bars carry no VIX); a day the VIX crosses 20 will show the step in the live point before the path.
  • The stored history behind the long version's chart is still version 2's; DS3's history for version 3 was not rebuilt here.
  • The pictures were taken against the live chart API through the local stand-in for Vercel; the deployed page was not measured, so the load time is this machine's (the HTML and the two modules are small; the live number needs the chart API's first answer).
