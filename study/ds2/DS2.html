The pass rule, written before any candidate was run: on the whole period, timing not more than 0.005 under today's and the worst fall not more than 1 point deeper; on his dates, at least one of the three (the two lows up, December down) better by a point and none worse by a point. (a) CREDIT AS CONTRARIAN AT ITS EXTREMES

Taken literally — a credit washout adds instead of subtracting — it changes nothing on his dates, because credit was not washed out on them (the high-yield fund's RSI was 43 and 39 at the two lows). The curve already adds once credit's own move is deeply negative. What cut the number was mild weakness, so the version that works is the other one: in a washed-out market, credit may add but not subtract. It costs nothing in the end result.

What it gives up, said plainly. Credit's caution was not empty. On the 228 washed-out days since 2018 (index RSI under 40), sorted by what credit was doing:

(b) DISTANCE BELOW THE 50-DAY AND THE 100-DAY AS AN ADD

It takes both lows to about 100%. It did not earn a vote by the deployment study's own bar: with SPY and QQQ under their 100-day, the median gain 60 sessions on was 1.84 points bigger (in 7 of 7 years) but gains came 5.2 points less often — bigger when it works, and it works less often. By itself its better end result (3.56×) came from holding 3.0 points more on average, not from timing. Added on top of version 2 it passes (3.53×, worst fall −29.8%, 84.8% invested), and on 2004 to 2009, which holds 2008, it cost 4.4% and deepened the worst fall by 2.4 points. It is the second decision at the foot of this page; it is not in the tool.

(c) A SMOOTHER NUMBER

A 3-day average halves the movement (0.79 points a day against 1.63) and costs 2.8% of the end result if every day's move were traded for free, 1.1% after the 0.05% charge; on version 2 the same average costs 3.6% and 2.2%. Against the 1% bar set beforehand that is too dear, by a little. The reading's edge is fast: most of what a dip day says, it says that day. "Quick to add, slow to cut" keeps that (it costs 0.5% after the charge) but trims the movement by 26%, short of the third asked. Neither is in the recommended version; it is the third decision.

3 · THE RECOMMENDED VERSION

1 · Credit does not subtract in a washed-out market. When the credit part would take points away, what it takes counts in full while SPY and QQQ's average RSI is 45 or more, not at all at 35 and under, and in proportion between. When credit adds, it adds in full.

2 · Cash is raised at an extended high and put back at the next washout. On at a close where SPY and QQQ are both within 2% of their highest close of 252 sessions and sit, on average, 11.21% or more above their 200-day averages (further than on four days in five). Off at a close where their average RSI is under 40. While it is on, the market reading is halved.

WHAT COULD BE WRONG

  • The raise-cash rule was shaped after the first results. The versions declared beforehand switched on and off every few sessions; the one that holds was added when that showed. It passed the same tests afterwards, but it was not written down first, and five versions of a top rule were tried in all.
  • Nine unseen years, all of them with sharp recoveries. Every panic since 2018 turned quickly. In 2004 to 2009 today's number and version 2 both did worse than buying and holding.
  • Version 2 is bolder exactly when credit is weak. On those days the market was lower a month later more often than not (46% higher). The late-March 2025 low is the example: 91% instead of 86% going into a 10.5% fall in two sessions. On the unseen years the days that read 80 or more go from 181 to 242; of them 65% were higher a month later (it was 74%) and 81% a quarter later (83%).
  • The raise-cash state remembers. One close a cent the other side of a threshold can change it for days: on at most 12 of 4,894 days in the test, none in the last year. The tool and the pane could disagree on such a day.
  • The tool holds 540 daily bars; the rule needs 252 before it can see a high. The tool takes the state of its first year from the stored list of stretches and works out the rest. A test holds its last year to the full history day for day; the list has to be rebuilt when the model is.
  • Looking back flatters it. The chart on this page is today's curves read on the years they were measured on. The whole-period figures are the unseen ones.
  • The odds table and the stretch rules in step 2 were measured on the reading before these changes and are still asked with it. They are right for what they say; they do not describe version 2's reading.
  • No costs, no taxes and no payouts in the replay, as in the deployment study.

DECISIONS FOR ALAN

  1. The raise-cash rule: on, or off? On, December 2025 reads 79% instead of 86% and nothing was lost over the unseen years; it also halves the response to a shallow dip like yesterday's, and it is on about a third of the time. Recommendation: on, and judge it on the pane, where one switch shows the line with and without it.
  2. Add at real pullbacks (depth under the 100-day): switch it on in a later round? It takes both lows to 100%. It did not earn a vote by the study's bar and it cost 4.4% through 2008. Recommendation: not yet — version 2 already reads 91 and 95% at the two lows.
  3. Steadier, at a price? A 3-day average halves the movement and costs about 2.2% of the end result over nine years after a small trading cost. Recommendation: leave it unsmoothed and look at version 2 for a week first; the two worst lurches (into the low, at the top) are the ones it removes.
PAGE SPECS

The whole-period test. The deployment study's own: the two parts' curves are fitted on every day from 10 March 2009 to the end of 2017 and the rule is replayed on 2 January 2018 to 6 October 2026. The share invested is set at a close and earns the next session's 50/50 blend of SPY and QQQ; cash earns the three-month bill; no costs, no payouts. A test in the repo holds this lab to the deployment study's stored answers (the same model to the last digit, the same 3.54×, −30.0% and 85.0%).

His dates. The late-March 2025 low is the last five sessions of March 2025 and the lowest SPY close among them (28 Mar 2025). The March 2026 low is 30 March and the four sessions before it. December 2025 is the month's 22 sessions; SPY's high before the fall is the highest close of the 120 sessions before the March 2026 low (27 Jan 2026, 695.49). "Moves a day" is the average day-to-day change from 7 Oct 2025 to 6 Oct 2026. These were fixed in the build before any candidate was run; today's number on them had been read, for the diagnosis.

Two fits. The tool and the pane use curves fitted on every day to July 2026, so "his dates" are shown that way: it is what he would see. The whole-period figures use the curves fitted to 2017 only. With those curves the same dates read: the 2026 low 98.0% (today's rule 93.0), December 78.8% (86.4).

The measured threshold. "11.21% above the 200-day" is the distance four days in five sat under, measured on the 4,362 days from 10 Mar 2009 to 13 Jul 2026; on the fitted years alone it is 10.05%, and the whole-period test uses that one. The other thresholds are round numbers fixed before the test. Moving each one a step changed the unseen end result between 3.45× and 3.60× (today's rule 3.54×).
