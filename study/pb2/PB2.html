“A stop that kicks out the position but buys lower” — workable, or too many ifs? As a one-candle rule: too many ifs, and it loses. The flush candle reached the lower buys (5% under the line) only 10% of the time — the other times it only kicked the position out, and the stock closed back over the line. As a daily-close rule it is workable, because every step is decided in the evening: (1) a close under the 100-day → out at the next open; (2) while out, three buys rest 5%, 6.5% and 8% under the line; (3) the first close back over it → buy back whatever those did not. Over the 2,780 breaches that version ended +2.6% on average against +3.3% for simply holding, the lower buys filled in 74% of cases, and the worst tenth ended −17.7% against −19.4% held. It gives back 77% of what the plain daily-close stop costs and keeps 37% of its protection in the worst tenth.

6 · What could be wrong

  • Daily bars only. A buy order “fills” when the session’s low reaches it, and an intraday stop when the low goes under it; inside the session the true order of prints is unknown. A session that both fills a buy and trips a stop is counted in that order. Slippage and the spread are not counted — an intraday stop in a fast flush would really sell lower than the line.
  • The pre-market and overnight sessions are not in the bars. The drafts are overnight-plus-next-day orders; a fill at 04:00 is priced here as a fill in the regular session.
  • Prices are split-adjusted, payouts are not added back. For Micron and most of these names that is small; for credit the dividend-adjusted series from FMP (read by the deployment study on 7 Oct 2026) is used.
  • The stocks are today’s survivors. 95 names that exist and matter now; leaders that later collapsed or were delisted are thin in it. That flatters every “60 sessions on” figure and every strategy without a stop. 9 names asked for were not served by the chart API (CELH, CMG, CVNA, DECK, FANG, LULU, NVO, TTD, ULTA).
  • Cases overlap. Several leaders pull back in the same weeks; the intervals resample whole calendar months to allow for that, but 2020–2021 and 2023–2026 still carry most of the cases (390 of 562 since 2016).
  • Micron’s own 48 set-ups use a wider net than the leader rule (no demand on SPY or the 200-day), and Micron’s 18 cases under the full rule are too few to stand alone. This year’s Micron — from 690 on its 200-day to a high of 1,255 on 25 Jun 2026 — is unlike most of its own past.
  • The two stop filters (a 3% band, two closes) were tried on the same cases they are judged on. They are candidates. The same goes for the “one usual day apart” spacing.
  • The stop with the deep bid can lose more than the planned money across its round trips: 2 of 522 cases did; the worst drew down −121% (Moderna from 20 Sep 2021: 5 stops, 4 deep fills, the stock itself −67% over the 120 sessions).
  • The regime model’s parameters are fitted on the whole span (2008–2026); only the reading of each day uses nothing later than that day. Four states fit better than three by the usual yardstick (29,431 against 31,894); three were kept because they can be named.
  • The volatility model reads Micron as unusually calm (3.1% a day, lower than on 99% of the past year’s sessions). The reach chances and the spacing lean on that; with the year’s middle swing (4.2%) every level is 26% closer in “usual days”.
  • The local models are small. They copy facts well and reason poorly; their critique is a cross-check on the obvious, nothing more.
  • No market rule has an interval clear of zero on SPY’s average. That is not proof they are wrong — 23 years of overlapping 60-session results hold far fewer independent periods than days.

A leader pullback (the coordinator’s rule of 7 Oct 2026, unchanged)

  • SPY closes at or over its 200-day, and the 200-day is higher than 10 sessions before.
  • The stock closes 25% or more over its own 200-day, itself higher than 10 sessions before.
  • The close is 8% or more under the highest high of the last 252 sessions.
  • The close is 0% to 3% over its 21-day, itself higher than 5 sessions before.
  • The 50-day and the 100-day are each higher than 5 sessions before. (The coordinator’s script asked for this too: with it its 19 names give its 185 cases; without it 196.)
  • A new case needs more than 10 sessions since the last evening that met the rule. “Bunched” adds: the 50- and 100-day within 3% of each other.
  • Micron’s own set-ups, used for the Micron replay only: the 8%-off-the-high and 21-day rules plus a rising 50- and 100-day; no demand on SPY or the 200-day.
  • All averages are simple averages of completed daily closes. “Touched” = a session’s low at or under that evening’s average; “fell to where it stood” = a low at or under the level the average had on the signal day.

Words used

  • The middle case is the median. The worst tenth is the 10th percentile. An interval is a 95% interval; “clear of zero” means the whole interval sits on one side of zero.
  • A session is a trading day. “20 sessions on” is the close 20 trading days after the signal day. A usual day is the middle high-to-low range the volatility model expects for the next session.
  • A whipsaw is a buy-back at a higher price than the sale before it. The planned money is the full position the plan intends — for Micron about 20% of the account.

The stops on breach days

  • A breach day for an average: the average is higher than 10 sessions before, the previous 20 closes were all at or over it, and today’s low is under the level the average had last evening (where a resting stop would sit). A new breach needs more than 10 sessions since the last.
  • A flush closes back at or over that evening’s average; a one-candle flush is a flush whose low is 5% or more under the previous close; a close break closes under it.
  • From one share held into the breach, each stop style is replayed for 60 sessions and scored against simply holding the share: the intraday stop sells at the line, or at the open when the session opens under it; the daily-close stop sells at the next open after a close under the average; both buy back at the open after the first close over it, and are armed again. The lower buys rest 5%, 6.5% and 8% under the average, a third of a share each, only while the share is out.

The order layer

  • Each evening the working buys are re-priced from that evening’s levels and stand for the next session only. At most three work at once: the three highest-priced rungs not yet filled.
  • A buy fills when the next session’s low reaches it — at its price, or at the open when the session opens under it.
  • Sizes are shares of the planned money (2 : 3 : 4 for the 21 / 50 / 100-day, the lowest level getting the most; 17.7 / 21.5 / 26.0 / 34.8% for the four drafts, which is 18 / 22 / 27 / 37 shares at their prices). Results are on the planned money, so money that never fills earns nothing.
  • The close stop: the evening the close is under that evening’s 100-day, everything is sold at the next open and the rungs rest. Buy-back: the first evening the close is over the 100-day, the same shares are bought at the next open. A whipsaw is a buy-back dearer than the sale before it.
  • The deep bid: while out, three buys 5%, 6.5% and 8% under the 100-day, a third of the planned money each, on evenings with the VIX at 20 or more. The fixed stop: 13% under the first fill, resting, never bought back.
  • The four drafts are replayed at fixed prices, scaled to each signal day’s close the way they sit under the 6 Oct close.

The open-source tools

  • arch 8.0.0 — the stationary block bootstrap behind every daily interval; GJR-GARCH(1,1) with Student-t errors on Micron’s last 2,520 daily moves for the usual day and the reach chances (the reach chances come from Micron’s own lows since 27 Sep 2016, each scaled by the model’s swing that day).
  • statsmodels 0.15.0 — the second interval for each edge (Newey–West errors for daily series, month-clustered errors for cases) and Wilson intervals for shares. statsmodels has no general bootstrap of its own, so the resampling is arch’s.
  • hmmlearn 0.3.3 — a three-state Gaussian hidden-Markov model on SPY’s day, SPY’s 20-session realised swing, the VIX and credit’s distance from its 200-day, 24 Jan 2008 to 6 Oct 2026; best of 12 starts; each day read with the forward filter only.
  • ruptures 1.1.10 — PELT with a continuous piecewise-linear cost on the log of Micron’s last 520 closes, at three strictness settings.
  • llama.cpp — Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct and Fin-o1-8B as 4-bit GGUF files on this MacBook; no paid model API. Each point must quote its fact word for word; quotes and figures are looked up in the sheet; ten yes/no questions are scored against the data.

What was not done

  • No intraday bars: the order of prints inside a session is not known.

  • The Lab’s stepping lines (2W D3, 3D C3, 1D D3) are not replayed through history: the packs hold today’s lines, and their pivots only from September 2025.
