2 · HOW MUCH TO INVEST — the ladder

The market's heat picks one of five rungs; the rung is the share of the money that goes to work, the rest stays in cash. The five numbers are yours (RISK POLICY in INPUTS).

3a · SECTORS — which sectors are overbought, which oversold: one blended reading per sector, in the Geiger's own look

One row per sector, most overbought at the top. The chip on the left is the cap-weighted reading (the index names), the chip on the right the equal-weighted reading (the average stock); they meet in the middle at the blend — the bow tie. Each chip reads like a name on the Hub's board: the fill to the right of the centre line is OVERBOUGHT (stretched), to the left is OVERSOLD (washed out). This step only says what each sector is doing; what that does to the money is the next step.

3b · HOW THE MONEY SPLITS — the % of equity per sector, computed from 3a with one stated rule

The rung (step 2) says how much of the money is invested. This step splits that invested part across the sectors from their 3a reading alone: an oversold sector earns more, an overbought one less, and the sector's own turn lifts or trims it. Every number in the table follows from the one on its left.

4 · THE COHORTS IN THOSE SECTORS — from the tree, as they are

The Hub's cohorts, grouped under the sector most of their names sit in, in the bow tie's order. Each shows how many names it holds, how many carry measured fundamentals, and the tree's reading. Pick a cohort and its names face off below.

5 · THE COMPS KNOCKOUT — inside the chosen cohort, business line by business line; the Geiger only says when

Only two names in the same business line may face each other (C5's lines: what each company actually sells — memory, data-center chips, semiconductor equipment …); a duel across business lines is impossible. Inside a line the names are seeded by fundamentals — four readings, each on its own scale against the line's field (the family when the line is thin), a peer priced strangely left out (C6b) — and first plays last until one is left, the line's champion. A duel is won on fundamentals; only when two names are even does the Geiger decide the timing. The champions stand on a podium, ordered by score, never duelling each other: the podium's first names are the cohort's picks — KEEP or DROP each; a decision is saved to the comps decisions table the moment you press it. The written concept: KNOCKOUT-CONCEPT (PA6) on the Hub.

6 · THE PICKS AND THEIR % — the allocation

What you kept, and what share of the money each gets: the rung (step 2) says how much is invested, 3b splits it across the sectors, and inside a sector the kept names split its share. The pie is the same one as the first screen.

7 · THE MOVES — what to do, split by schedule

The gap between where you are and the target mix, per sleeve, split into tranches you can actually execute.

8 · MOVE OPTIONS — every name in the sectors the plan funds, by category

The names inside the sectors the model funds, from the Hub's cohorts — the sectors whose aggregate is statistically sound come first. Ranked fundamentals first (price, growth, margin and leverage against each name's own peers, outliers out), then the Geiger for timing. The first three in each sector are tagged ADD. Star a name to send it to COMPARABLES.

8b · COMPARABLES — the short list and the comps sheet

The ADD names, lined up like a comps sheet with median/mean/min/max — judge relative value before acting. Each name is compared with its own named peers (FMP and Massive), far-out multiples dropped the way the Hub's comps tab does it; TARGET is the median of the checked analyst targets (one per firm, last 90 days) and UPSIDE is that against today's price. Cells are shaded vs the peer median: green = better than peers, orange = worse (cheaper P/E, higher margins & growth, lower debt = better). Drop greys a row and pulls it out of the stats without deleting it; pick marks your keepers (capped at your TOTAL-names hard cap). Star more names in the table above to add non-suggested candidates.

PAGE SPECS

The sources line (PA6, 6 Oct). Under the title, one line says how many of the page's sources read live, how many are old and how many could not be reached; "details" opens the list. A source is LIVE when it was read just now and is within the age that source is allowed (the Geiger thirty minutes, the quotes fifteen, a profile a week, the sector ranking three hours); OLD when the read worked but the newest row is older than that; UNREACHABLE when the read failed, and the row then says what the page uses instead (an older table, or a voter that abstains). Nothing is faked: an unreachable source never shows a number.


The knockout (PA6). Built to KNOCKOUT-CONCEPT.html on the Hub and to nothing else. Who may face whom: only two companies in the same business line — C5's lines (lines.mjs, imported from the Hub as it is) over FMP's revenue by segment and the company profiles, each company placed in the line that carries most of its revenue; one hand rule added here (AMAT, LRCX, KLAC, ASML are semiconductor equipment). How a duel is scored: four readings (price = P/E or P/S for a loss-maker, revenue growth, net margin, leverage), each the name's position between the first and third quartile of the line's field (the family when the line has fewer than five measured names; the card says which), C6b's price-outliers out of the field first; the score is the average, a missing reading counting one half; a duel is won on fundamentals when the scores are at least 0.02 apart, otherwise the Geiger decides the timing. Seeds by score inside the line, first plays last, rounds until one is left; a lone name is champion unopposed and says so. The champions stand on a podium by score and never duel; the podium's first names are the picks. The fundamentals for every served company are loaded so each line has its field.


3a and 3b (PA6). Step 3 is two screens. 3a SECTORS says what each sector is doing and nothing else: the blended reading per sector (five readings averaged, the weights in INPUTS), drawn in the Hub's own Geiger chip — the same flat bar the Hub's board uses, the centre line at zero, the fill to the right when positive (overbought, stretched) and to the left when negative (oversold, washed out), green and red as on the Hub. The cap-weighted chip on the left and the equal-weighted chip on the right meet at the blend: that is the bow tie. The words are the ladder's own five rungs. 3b HOW THE MONEY SPLITS takes those readings and nothing else and shows, column by column, how the invested part is split: coldness = (1 − reading) ÷ 2 (never below 0.05); × the sector's turn (improving ×1.35, pullback inside an uptrend ×0.85, breakdown ×0.6, leading ×1); normalised; each sector held between 2% and 35% and renormalised; × the rung's % invested. The last column is the % of equity the pie and the picks use.


What changed on 5 Oct (PA4). Two breadth voters: ADVANCE / DECLINE is the line over the names the Hub serves to this tool — one up or down per name per completed session from the chart API's daily bars, summed over the last 20 sessions, plus today's session from the live quotes; the vote is that line's slope, scaled so two thirds of the names rising every day reads +1 (default weight 0.5). CONCENTRATION is the equal-weight twin (RSP) minus the cap-weight index (SPY) over the same 20 sessions, scaled so a five-point spread reads ±1, with the share of the served set's cap-weighted move that its ten largest names carried (default weight 0.5). The VIX votes live from the chart API's macro board; the VIX term structure (spot ÷ 3-month) uses the VIX table's last close for the 3-month leg because no live 3-month VIX exists in the estate, and says that date on its dial.


The sector method dial. TARGET MIX reads each sector one of four ways: STATE STREET SECTORS (the sector fund's own Geiger), HUB SECTOR COMPARE (the fund and its equal-weight twin, averaged), MARKET BOW TIE (the average served stock in the sector, every name equal-weight, live from the chart API, with the bow tie against the cap-weighted read), TREE READINGS (the tree's close tier, 5,316 common stocks and ADRs, rolled up by sector — 652 of them carry a sector in the estate today). The default is a mix, 40 / 20 / 20 / 20 in that order; the weights are dials in INPUTS. The pie, the moves and the ranking all follow the dial. Crypto, metals and oil have no fund, so every option reads them from their own constituents.


The short list. For the five sectors the tool favours most (sound sectors first, then by share), the ADD names and the two best of each one's comps field — the business-first peers from the peer table, same sector and within ten times its size (C5's rule), ranked by fundamentals — as the approved cards. KEEP / DROP appends one row to the comps decisions table through the public write path the Hub's comps tab uses; the latest row per company and peer is the state. An ADD tag needs at least two measured fundamentals.


What goes on the Hub. A pick, a name on his favourites, or a KEEP decision is tagged NEEDS THE LIVE HUB; a comps peer without those is FUNDAMENTALS ONLY — OFF-HUB IS ENOUGH. The list is written as control/ALLOCATION-HUB-GUIDANCE.json (ticker · tag · reason · date) for the served-set decision.


The fold. Every section is folded to one screen (1680 × 1050: about 840 px of body, scrolling inside) with a sticky section bar; THE BRIEF and the TARGET MIX pie share the first screen; INPUTS opens as a drawer over the page. Nothing was removed; the fold state is remembered on this device.
