What changed on the evening of 6 Oct (AL7) — pictures in place of paragraphs. THE BRIEF is one sentence, one gauge, two bars and at most six bullets. The gauge is the market's heat on its usual scale with the ladder's five rungs drawn as bands (the edges at −0.5, −0.2, +0.2 and +0.5 are the lines between them), today's needle, the % invested under each band, and under it the two edges that would change the rung with how far away each is. The two bars are the two camps: every voting row above zero against every voting row below, each with its weighted average and the share of the vote it carries. The sentences those pictures replace are kept, folded, under "the same in words" in step 1.


The voters, by family. Every row sits in one of five families: INDEX (the S&P, the Nasdaq, small caps, semis, the eleven sectors blended), BREADTH (equal-weight against cap-weight in every pair, advance/decline, our companies above their 200-day and 50-day, concentration, small against large, TRIN, up against down volume), RATES & CREDIT (the 10-year, the curve, high yield, long bonds, and oil, which votes as inflation pressure), SENTIMENT (the VIX, its term structure, the put/call, bitcoin as risk appetite), DEFENSIVES (staples and utilities, gold). A family is drawn as one bar — the weighted average of its voting rows — with its share of the vote; press it for its rows. A row's share of the vote is its weight divided by the weight of every voting row, and that number says whether the row is used: a row at 0% is a reading only, and a row with no reading or one 30 days old sits in the folded list below with its reason. The heat is the sum of each family's share times its average — the same weighted average as before.


What votes now that did not. (1) Breadth reads every pair: the equal-weight fund's Geiger minus the cap-weight fund's for the S&P (RSP − SPY), the Nasdaq-100 (QQQE − QQQ) and the eleven sectors, averaged; it took the weight the single S&P pair carried, and that pair stays under it as a reading. Inside ±0.05 a pair counts as level. (2) The share of our own companies — the ones in the tree, about 450 — whose last close is above their own 200-day average, and the same against the 50-day: half above reads 0, all +1, none −1; weight 0.25 each. It is our universe, not the whole market. (3) The Interactive Brokers put/call: its place among its own measured sessions, turned so a low put/call (little fear) is the hot side; while it has fewer than 20 other sessions the vote is scaled by sessions ÷ 20, and under three it does not vote; weight 0.25. On a device that already holds saved dials only those weights were changed, once.


Sectors that know the tree. Step 3a shows the eleven sectors, crypto and metals as before, and under them the cohorts of the tree's AI heading — the tree is read from its two tables as it stands, so a revision of the tree shows by itself. A cohort is read three ways, each on the Geiger scale, averaged with equal weight: its members each counted the same, its members by market value, and the fund the tree names for it. Metals are read from the metal: the bullion trusts for gold and silver on the chart API's Geiger are the reading; the futures row from the Hub's second source is shown beside it and never averaged in, because it is a different computation and the two can disagree widely; copper has no commodity line in the estate and is named as missing; the miners are the folded detail in the metals branch and never enter the reading. "How the blend is made" draws the five methods of every sector side by side with the blend, the spread from the highest method to the lowest as a bar, and the number of companies that sit in every method's set (the fund's holdings, the names we serve, the tree's close tier, the membership the ranking averages). A cell more than 0.20 from the blend — half the middle rung's width — is ringed, and a spread of 0.40 or more, a whole rung, is marked "they disagree".


Branches. Pressing a row in 3a opens its branch: the funds to track (the State Street fund, the Invesco equal-weight twin, and the Vanguard and iShares near copies the tree lists; for a tree cohort the funds the tree names; for metals the trusts) and the names that pass the comps filter — at least two measured fundamentals readings and a fundamentals score of 0.50 or more against the name's own peers, price outliers out first — best first. Each name carries its growth (the consensus revenue of the next fiscal year against the reported year before, from the stored analyst estimates; the comps feed's growth is not shown because it changes from call to call), its Geiger, and its nearest reviewed line above and below the price with the Lab's own label. The lines come from the Lab's extract of 6 Oct, kept as a dated file beside the page (19 names); a sloped line moves a little each session, so every level carries that date. Where the Hub serves a decision card for a name (CP1), the card's lines are used.


Few sleeves, core and conviction. The rung says how much is invested. A stated share of that — a fifth by default, R4's figure for a market in a range — is CONVICTION: it belongs to the names kept in the knockout, whatever their sector, sized by the score that ranks them; until a name is kept it waits and the page says so once. The rest is the CORE: half of it by default is the index sleeve, held as the equal-weight S&P (RSP), and the other half goes to a few sector or tree sleeves held through their funds. The candidates are ranked by the rule of 5 Oct, unchanged — coldness = the larger of 0.05 and (1 − reading) ÷ 2, × the turn (improving ×1.35, pullback inside an uptrend ×0.85, breakdown ×0.6, leading ×1); only the first few are funded — at most "most sleeves" less the index and conviction sleeves — and the smallest is dropped until none is under the smallest sleeve allowed. Those two rules replace the 2%–35% rails. The four numbers are dials in INPUTS and are proposals. The full ranking, with every number that puts a sleeve in or out, is folded under the bar.


Small caps or breadth. The index sleeve is the equal-weight S&P, not small caps: the bet is that the average large stock catches up with its index (breadth returning). The two reads are shown side by side from the same daily bars — the Geiger gap to the S&P, the distance off the high of the last 225 sessions, the 50-day and 200-day, the RSI. The count in the last row is R4's, dated 5 Oct: of the sessions of the last two years that looked like today (middling heat, narrow leadership), how often the S&P beat the equal-weight S&P over the next 60. Small caps were not scored in that study. The older large/small lens no longer moves any money.


The regime step's mechanics (R3, 6 Oct 2026). PUT / CALL is the Interactive Brokers collector's own count (puts ÷ calls over the served names, one row per session): the row shows the newest session that measured anything, with its date, how many names were measured and its age; a session where the gateway was logged out measures nothing and is named. (That morning it carried weight 0 and no level; since the evening it votes on its own sessions, scaled down while they are few — see above.) A row with no reading, or whose newest reading is 30 or more days old, cannot vote: it sits in the folded "not voting — why" list under the voters with its reason and date, and its weight is kept. The sentence under the heat splits the voters into the camp above zero and the camp below, each with its weighted average — hot weight × hot average plus cold weight × cold average, over all the weight, is the heat. "Against its own past" replays the same formulas over the stored readings: every voter with a stored past, only on evenings when all of them are there, and separately the four with a full year; today is measured on the same rows it is compared with, the count of evenings is always given, and under 60 evenings the line says THIN. The sector count in the heat row and in the brief is one function: the eleven State Street sectors, each on its blended trend and momentum.


The sources line (PA6, 6 Oct). Under the title, one line says how many of the page's sources read live, how many are old and how many could not be reached; "details" opens the list. A source is LIVE when it was read just now and is within the age that source is allowed (the Geiger thirty minutes, the quotes fifteen, a profile a week, the sector ranking three hours); OLD when the read worked but the newest row is older than that; UNREACHABLE when the read failed, and the row then says what the page uses instead (an older table, or a voter that abstains). Nothing is faked: an unreachable source never shows a number.


The knockout (PA6). Built to KNOCKOUT-CONCEPT.html on the Hub and to nothing else. Who may face whom: only two companies in the same business line — C5's lines (lines.mjs, imported from the Hub as it is) over FMP's revenue by segment and the company profiles, each company placed in the line that carries most of its revenue; one hand rule added here (AMAT, LRCX, KLAC, ASML are semiconductor equipment). How a duel is scored: four readings (price = P/E or P/S for a loss-maker, revenue growth, net margin, leverage), each the name's position between the first and third quartile of the line's field (the family when the line has fewer than five measured names; the card says which), C6b's price-outliers out of the field first; the score is the average, a missing reading counting one half; a duel is won on fundamentals when the scores are at least 0.02 apart, otherwise the Geiger decides the timing. Seeds by score inside the line, first plays last, rounds until one is left; a lone name is champion unopposed and says so. The champions stand on a podium by score and never duel; the podium's first names are the picks. The fundamentals for every served company are loaded so each line has its field.


3a and 3b (PA6 — as built that morning; the rails and the pie it mentions were replaced that evening, see above). Step 3 is two screens. 3a SECTORS says what each sector is doing and nothing else: the blended reading per sector (five readings averaged, the weights in INPUTS), drawn in the Hub's own Geiger chip — the same flat bar the Hub's board uses, the centre line at zero, the fill to the right when positive (overbought, stretched) and to the left when negative (oversold, washed out), green and red as on the Hub. The cap-weighted chip on the left and the equal-weighted chip on the right meet at the blend: that is the bow tie. The words are the ladder's own five rungs. 3b HOW THE MONEY SPLITS takes those readings and nothing else and shows, column by column, how the invested part is split: coldness = (1 − reading) ÷ 2 (never below 0.05); × the sector's turn (improving ×1.35, pullback inside an uptrend ×0.85, breakdown ×0.6, leading ×1); normalised; each sector held between 2% and 35% and renormalised; × the rung's % invested. The last column is the % of equity the pie and the picks use.


What changed on 5 Oct (PA4). Two breadth voters: ADVANCE / DECLINE is the line over the names the Hub serves to this tool — one up or down per name per completed session from the chart API's daily bars, summed over the last 20 sessions, plus today's session from the live quotes; the vote is that line's slope, scaled so two thirds of the names rising every day reads +1 (default weight 0.5). CONCENTRATION is the equal-weight twin (RSP) minus the cap-weight index (SPY) over the same 20 sessions, scaled so a five-point spread reads ±1, with the share of the served set's cap-weighted move that its ten largest names carried (default weight 0.5). The VIX votes live from the chart API's macro board; the VIX term structure (spot ÷ 3-month) uses the VIX table's last close for the 3-month leg because no live 3-month VIX exists in the estate, and says that date on its dial.


The sector method dial. TARGET MIX reads each sector one of four ways: STATE STREET SECTORS (the sector fund's own Geiger), HUB SECTOR COMPARE (the fund and its equal-weight twin, averaged), MARKET BOW TIE (the average served stock in the sector, every name equal-weight, live from the chart API, with the bow tie against the cap-weighted read), TREE READINGS (the tree's close tier, 5,316 common stocks and ADRs, rolled up by sector — 652 of them carry a sector in the estate today). The default is a mix, 40 / 20 / 20 / 20 in that order; the weights are dials in INPUTS. The pie, the moves and the ranking all follow the dial. Crypto, metals and oil have no fund, so every option reads them from their own constituents.


The short list. For the five sectors the tool favours most (sound sectors first, then by share), the ADD names and the two best of each one's comps field — the business-first peers from the peer table, same sector and within ten times its size (C5's rule), ranked by fundamentals — as the approved cards. KEEP / DROP appends one row to the comps decisions table through the public write path the Hub's comps tab uses; the latest row per company and peer is the state. An ADD tag needs at least two measured fundamentals.


What goes on the Hub. A pick, a name on his favourites, or a KEEP decision is tagged NEEDS THE LIVE HUB; a comps peer without those is FUNDAMENTALS ONLY — OFF-HUB IS ENOUGH. The list is written as control/ALLOCATION-HUB-GUIDANCE.json (ticker · tag · reason · date) for the served-set decision.


The fold. Every section is folded to one screen (1680 × 1050: about 840 px of body, scrolling inside) with a sticky section bar; THE BRIEF and the money bar share the first screen; INPUTS opens as a drawer over the page. Nothing was removed; the fold state is remembered on this device.
